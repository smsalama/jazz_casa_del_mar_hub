## Context that shapes every row

1. **Speculative concept.** The site is an unofficial redesign concept of the website of a real resort (Jaz Elite Casa del Mar Beach, Hurghada, Egypt, part of the JAZ Hotel Group). JAZ has not commissioned or authorised it (brief, Decision 2).
2. **Local review only.** The release is a package run on one computer (FR-47), with synthetic data only (NFR-26), every page marked as a concept (FR-17) and set to noindex (NFR-27). In that state no real visitor uses it and no real personal data is processed. Most obligations below therefore bite only if the concept is ever hosted where other people can reach it (OQ-13), or adopted by the resort. The rows still record them, because the requirements are written so that they hold if hosted.
3. **Nothing is built yet.** No row can be "Verified against documented criteria". The best status available now is "Partially verified", meaning that a requirement in `docs/requirements.md` specifies a control that would satisfy the obligation once built and tested.
4. **Recent UK law changes.** Several provisions changed during 2025 and 2026 under the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (DUAA). The legislation.gov.uk pages opened on 2026-10-15 show, among others: PECR regulation 6 substituted and Schedule A1 inserted (5 February 2026); the controller complaints duty in DPA 2018 section 164A fully in force (19 June 2026); and the regulator's name in DPA 2018 section 165 and UK GDPR Article 30 changed to the Information Commission (30 September 2026). Several ICO guidance pages opened say they are under review because of the DUAA. Rows citing ICO guidance should be re-checked before any hosted use.

## Status values

- **Verified against documented criteria**: built, tested and evidenced. Not used yet.
- **Partially verified**: a control is specified in the requirements; not yet built or tested.
- **Remediation required**: the requirements have a gap; the row names the FR or NFR to change.
- **Not applicable**: with the reason given.
- **Requires qualified legal advice**: uncertain; sent to Steven's solicitor in the question pack.

"Applies" values: yes; no (with reason); uncertain.

## Research view on the legal open questions

These are inputs for Steven, not decisions. Each one names the register rows it rests on.

**OQ-5 Editing versus concept marking and noindex.** The working answer (notice wording editable per language, never empty, cannot be hidden, no noindex setting) supports LR-25, LR-33 and LR-34. A gap remains: an editor could reword the notice into something misleading (for example "Official site"). Suggest a fixed, non-editable core sentence in each language ("Unofficial design concept. Not affiliated with or endorsed by JAZ Hotels.") with an optional editable second sentence. Keep noindex outside any admin setting.

**OQ-8 Machine translation and legal pages.** Machine assisted German, Italian and Russian text is acceptable for a concept reviewed locally with synthetic data, because nobody relies on it. Before any hosting, the privacy, cookie and terms pages in each language should be checked by a qualified translator and, for the privacy notice, by the solicitor or the controller, because Art 12(1) requires information that the people addressed can understand (LR-6). The content register in FR-16 is the right control.

**OQ-10 Retention periods.** See the research view on the retention schedule below (LR-8).

**OQ-12 Data controller and lawful basis.** For local review with synthetic data there is, in practice, no real personal data and no controller decision to make. For any hosted instance, the controller is whoever decides why and how enquiry and subscriber data is used (S28). The privacy notice must not name JAZ or the resort as controller unless they have agreed, because that would be a false statement (LR-25). A marked placeholder is right for now. Suggested bases, to be confirmed in Q7: enquiries, Art 6(1)(b) (steps at the person's request before a contract) or 6(1)(f) legitimate interests, without a required consent tick (LR-2); newsletter, consent under Art 6(1)(a) and PECR reg 22 (LR-19); admin accounts, the staff relationship (contract or legitimate interests); security logging and rate limiting, legitimate interests (Annex 1 recognised legitimate interests were not read, so not relied on). The data protection fee, UK representative and transfer rules all follow from who the controller is (LR-15, LR-16, LR-17).

**OQ-13 Local review versus hosting.** Local review only is the lowest risk position and should stay the rule until Q1 to Q3 are answered. Hosting anywhere reachable by others (even password protected for a pitch) changes LR-1, LR-25, LR-32, LR-33, LR-34 and LR-46 from theoretical to live. Suggest a written hosting condition: no hosting until the solicitor's answers are recorded here, FR-44, FR-46 and FR-33 are promoted to Must, and the remediation rows are closed.

**OQ-15 Book now target.** See LR-46. For the review package, a placeholder URL avoids sending anyone to the live engine. If the live engine or group page is kept, a visible label that it is an external site not connected with the concept is safer than no interstitial. The final position is Q2.

**OQ-16 Contact details and social accounts.** Agree with the working answer: synthetic placeholders for telephone, WhatsApp, email, address and social links, and a Google Maps link to the public location only (LR-47).

**OQ-17 JAZ name, wordmark and badges.** Agree that no badge, award, certification or review mark appears (LR-26, LR-27: displaying a trust or quality mark without authorisation is unfair in all circumstances). Showing the resort name as typeset text still uses the name, which is the core of any trade mark or passing off concern; typesetting reduces copyright risk in logo artwork but not trade mark or passing off risk. Whether the name can appear in headings and titles of a speculative concept is Q1. A neutral codename for the concept (with the real name only in the "About this concept" text) is a lower risk fallback if the solicitor advises against using the name.

**OQ-19 Factual claims in placeholder copy.** Agree with the working answer (no numeric facts beyond counts of named items, no prices, star rating, awards or review quotes). Extend it to testable qualitative superlatives ("award winning", "best beach in Hurghada", "largest pool on the Red Sea"), which also need evidence (CAP 3.7, LR-30), and to offers: no deadlines or scarcity claims (LR-29).
